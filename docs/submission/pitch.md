# Pitch (10 minutes)

Spoken script for the deck ([PDF](https://github.com/himoji/kassa/blob/main/docs/submission/kassa-deck.pdf), [online version](https://claude.ai/artifact/Sdktm1BZ9157k6Lj1qa4Y7)), one row per slide. Limit 10 minutes; the script runs about 9:20 (about 1,100 words plus a 60-second video cut). No live clicks. Slide 7 plays a cut of the [demo video](./demo-script.md).

- Pitch line: "Kassa's agent pays your bills across borders on its own, inside the limits you signed."
- Safety line: "The agent can prepare anything, but only your signature, now or in a mandate signed earlier, can move your money."

Every figure is in the repository; do not round up. "Sixteen attacks" means A1 to A8 and A10 to A17 (`docs/evidence/attacks.json`). A9, swapped bank details on a supplier invoice, is tested separately (refused, nothing paid) but left out of the 16: it is a small-business flow with no baseline to compare (`apps/api/test/naive/harness.ts`). Cases are REFUSED (code stopped it), ASKED (passkey prompt on the exact order; a fooled user could approve) or PAID. Say "asks", never "blocked", for ASKED.

## Elevator version (30 seconds)

International students live in two currencies: money arrives from home in one, bills are due abroad in another. Kassa is an AI agent that does that money work for them. It reads their bank statements, plans rent and tuition against a forecast, picks the cheapest route and pays. Inside a mandate the student signed with a passkey it pays on its own; anything else it prepares as an exact order for the student to sign. Code, not the model, decides which is which, so we recreated sixteen attacks on AI agents with money, eight of them from real incidents. Kassa refuses seven outright and stops eight at a passkey prompt; one, a QR code swapped inside the wallet after Kassa's check, gets through and is only detected. A chatbot whose payment tool needs just a confirm tap pays the attacker in eleven of sixteen once the fooled user taps; Kassa, with a user who approves every prompt, in five.

## Questions to expect

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is any real money moved? | No. Sandbox rails, a mock bank, fictitious companies. In production the wallet sits with a licensed bank or e-money institution; Kassa is the agent and rules on top. |
| What if the model is jailbroken? | We assume it is; the attack suite uses models that obey the attacker. The model only proposes; code decides. It pays alone only inside a mandate or small-payment limit you signed; everything else needs your passkey over the exact order hash, and payments re-checks the signature. A fooled user can still approve, so new payees wait 24 hours and are never paid automatically. |
| Why not let the user approve everything? | Then it is a form, not an agent. Mandates pay rent and insurance to verified payees with no prompt; the risk score keeps prompts for cases that need them. |
| Which model do you use? | Any provider through one interface; the deployment uses a small, cheap model (`PLANNER_MODEL`, `READER_MODEL`). Without a key the chat answers from recorded replays labelled "Replay". |
| How accurate is the Reader? | 21 of 30 labelled invoices exact on every field; per field 83 % (reference) to 97 % (due date), median 16 s (`docs/evidence/invoice-eval.json`, measured on `deepseek-v4.1-flash`; the current default `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` has not been re-run). The six values it got wrong but still verified are all rejected by the stricter checks added the next day. A wrong account number cannot pay: the payee must be verified and out of cooling. |
| Credit record: who trusts it? | It is a signed, verifiable statement of ledger facts, not a score. A landlord can check it was not edited. Bureau reporting needs a licensed partner. |
| What about China? | Region CN: Kassa prepares the payment and hands it to the user's QR wallet after checking the payee name; a separate PIPL cross-border consent is required before any model outside the server sees data (`docs/security/compliance.md`). |
| Blockchain? | Signed ledger checkpoints are anchored on Sepolia: two transactions whose input data is the checkpoint hash (`docs/evidence/anchors.json`, Etherscan links in the README). Re-check with `bun run ledger:anchor:verify --no-db`. No money or personal data on chain. |
| Why iPhone only? | Only Apple Pay capture is iPhone-specific (iOS lets no app read other apps' notifications, so a Shortcut does it). The web app runs on any phone, Android captures through a notification forwarder (Settings → Payment taps), and the judge build needs no phone. |
| What is not done? | Real rails, an external pentest, a hardware key store; some stores are in memory. See `docs/security/self-assessment.md` §7. |
